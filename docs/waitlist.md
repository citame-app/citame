# Salon waitlist and temporary offers

The waitlist is for the single Sisters of Beauty location. It defaults off. The owner can enable it from **Dashboard → Waitlist** and choose an offer validity from 5 to 120 minutes; the default is 30. Pausing stops new entries and offers. Existing unexpired offers keep their deadline and can still be accepted.

A customer joins from their account or the booking time step. That link carries the selected services, professional and date. Choose up to eight distinct services, a preferred professional or any qualified professional, up to 30 consecutive calendar dates, and a daily earliest-start/latest-finish window. The complete blocked service time, including cleanup buffers, must fit that window. Dates must fit the salon's normal booking horizon. A customer can have at most five active requests; duplicate active selections are rejected.

Offers are checked by the API process every minute, independently of email/payment configuration. Each run scans at most 20 waiting entries in rotating batches and releases at most 100 expired or unavailable offers. A long queue may take several passes. This is a bounded rotating queue, not a strict global first-come guarantee across concurrent workers. Normal service qualification, working hours, time off, notice, active profile and booking horizon rules apply. An offer cannot extend into the minimum-notice period.

A valid offer temporarily removes that professional's slot from availability and prevents ordinary bookings/reschedules from taking it. The customer sees its professional, services, price, timezone and exact acceptance deadline in their account. Only the authorized customer or a permitted operator can accept it. Acceptance creates an ordinary appointment atomically; repeated attempts with the same request key return the original booking. The time is never booked twice. Changes to the displayed service or deposit terms require declining the outdated offer and waiting for a fresh one.

The customer explicitly accepts an applicable deposit/cancellation policy before booking. Managers may record the same explicit deposit waiver as for an ordinary booking. Reception cannot waive it. A protected appointment remains pending until its deposit is actually funded and sends no confirmation before funding. A waitlist offer itself neither charges a card nor creates a subscription. Redsys activation still requires the real bank configuration.

Declining an offer or allowing it to expire releases the slot and pauses that request for 15 minutes. At most three offers are made per customer per salon-local calendar day. Requests whose date window has passed expire. A blocked/deleted profile, inactive professional, archived service or removed professional qualification withdraws an unaccepted offer and closes the unavailable request. Existing accepted appointments follow the normal booking controls and are not cancelled merely by pausing the waitlist.

Customers can cancel an active request with an explicit confirmation. This releases its offer and leaves existing appointments unchanged. Each converted request links to the actual booking and its current calendar date. Professionals may read unassigned waiting requests and manage only entries on their own calendar; managers/reception see permitted salon-wide entries. A replay cannot expose an accepted appointment that was subsequently moved off a professional's calendar. Internal access-control reasons never appear in customer responses.

**Delivery scope:** offers currently appear in the authenticated account, which refreshes every 15 seconds and has an explicit refresh action. Joining is not marketing consent. Email/SMS offer delivery is part of the still-open durable messaging/marketing work and is not represented as sent. The scheduling roadmap also retains constrained resources, service variants/add-ons and processing gaps as separate work.

Run `bun run --cwd apps/web e2e:waitlist` after building the web app. It starts the explicit local fixture, uses separate test customers to respect offer limits, exercises actual Postgres/API/browser behavior and simulates only expiry. No bank request or real message is sent. The fixture's private control routes are absent from the production executable.
