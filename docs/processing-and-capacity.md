# Processing time and professional capacity

The salon starts with one concurrent client per professional and continuous hands-on work for every treatment. Owners/managers configure processing in **Services → Processing time** and concurrent capacity in the selected **Team** member's profile. The Services page has direct links to combinations and processing settings.

Processing is a hands-off middle interval inside the treatment's existing total duration. Enter positive whole minutes of initial work and processing, leaving at least one minute of final work. A 90-minute treatment with 30 minutes of initial work and 30 minutes of processing needs the professional during minutes 0–30 and 60–90. Its cleanup buffer remains blocked afterwards. The customer's displayed visit duration and price stay unchanged. Changing a treatment's duration cannot leave an invalid processing interval; adjust/remove processing before shortening it.

The professional can take another compatible appointment during processing. Rooms, equipment and stations retain their original full service window, including processing and cleanup. Releasing a professional does not release a chair occupied by the first client. The full visit must still fit the professional's applicable weekly/dated working hours, time off, notice and advance limits. Processing does not allow a visit to cross a working-hours break or scheduled time off.

Concurrent capacity is a separate explicit choice from one to six clients during active work. Changing the value requires a new confirmation. The limit applies to active intervals, not the number of clients physically present during processing. A lower limit is rejected while future work or unexpired held offers cannot fit. No appointment is moved, cancelled, repriced or shortened by a policy change. Interchangeable internal unit numbers can change; physical resource assignments follow their own rules.

New appointments capture an immutable work plan plus their treatment snapshots. Catalog changes apply to new bookings. Existing visits keep their original hands-on/processing times and buffers when moved to another time or qualified professional. Legacy appointments remain continuously blocked, even if processing is later enabled on their old treatment. The calendar marks processing intervals and shows their saved times in the appointment details. Customer choices and booking summaries disclose included processing time.

Waitlist offers use the same professional capacity as appointments. An offer can fit within another visit's processing interval. Unexpired offers reserve only their own active work; expiry or withdrawal releases that capacity. Acceptance withdraws its own hold inside the booking transaction before allocating the resulting appointment, preserving the existing actor/key/reviewed-terms controls. Expired holds are released under the salon scheduling lock without taking queue-entry locks in reverse order.

Configuration writes are versioned. Stale responses preserve the visible draft. An unknown response freezes its exact reviewed packet for retry or explicit reload; a successful first write can make the old revision stale on retry, so reload the saved configuration before editing again. Drafts are held in memory, not browser storage. Field changes and new revisions require fresh review/confirmation.

The desktop replica retains processing metadata and current professional capacity in its JSON rows without resetting the existing cursor or queue. New proposals freeze their reviewed service names, durations, processing, prices and quote hashes. Later catalog pulls cannot rewrite that pending proposal. The local snapshot is stripped from the wire payload; the normal server quote fence rejects changed terms. An older mirror remains readable but must sync quote hashes before creating a new proposal. When reviewed terms are rejected, merely moving the proposal cannot accept them: discard it and review a new booking. Existing queued legacy packets remain readable and retain their original replay behavior.

## Database and deployment

Apply migrations before starting the updated API. `0037` adds the frozen timing columns and internal `staff_reservation` rows, backfills legacy appointments/offers, and replaces the full client-span exclusion with a deferred GiST exclusion on professional/unit/active-work interval. It retains the `appointment_no_overlap` constraint name. Owner triggers derive reservation staff, times, active state and offsets from the immutable parent plan. Deferred completeness checks prevent independent removal of a visit's occupancy. Policy and slot guards enforce current capacity; historical work before a policy's effective time can retain its original labels. `0038` serializes direct SQL policy updates with the same calendar lock as allocation triggers.

Application writers preserve receipt → salon scheduling → ordered calendars → client/value → parent → child allocation ordering. Professional unit numbers are bookkeeping, so recolouring changes no physical resource or client assignment. Availability uses a pooled peak-demand calculation; fragmented older unit labels cannot hide a feasible future interval. Rescheduling keeps the saved work offsets. The API, delegated booking and offline replay share these domain services. Internal occupancy rows are not part of the desktop sync protocol.

The contention proof now checks active professional work units and configured limits. It reports full client-span overlaps separately; those can be legitimate after this feature. Use `bun k6/check-overlaps.ts` against the intended migrated database.

Verification includes actual competing bookings, raw SQL backstops and lock waits, reduced-capacity rejection, frozen catalog/move behavior, occupied rooms during exposure, waitlist hold/acceptance, desktop persistence and reviewed-terms conflicts, plus browser accessibility and overflow. All fixture clients, visits and treatments are synthetic. There are no bank or external message operations in these checks.
